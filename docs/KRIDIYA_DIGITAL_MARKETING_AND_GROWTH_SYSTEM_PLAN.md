# Kridiya Travel Digital Marketing and Growth System

Status: Proposed master plan
Prepared: 2026-07-28
Business: Kridiya Travel and Tourism FZ-LLC, Ras Al Khaimah, UAE
Brand promise: **Your Journey, Our Passion.**

## 1. Executive decision

Kridiya should not market itself as another self-service online booking engine. Its strongest position is:

> **A responsive UAE travel advisor that compares practical options, explains the conditions clearly, and personally manages the journey from enquiry to confirmation.**

Marketing must sell three things before it sells a destination:

1. **Trust** — a real UAE business, clear terms, secure handling, honest quotations.
2. **Speed** — fast human response on WhatsApp, phone, and email.
3. **Care** — one accountable advisor across flights, hotels, visas, holidays, Umrah, cruises, and corporate travel.

The complete system is:

`Audience -> campaign -> tracked visit/message -> enquiry -> qualified lead -> quote -> follow-up -> payment -> booking -> delivery -> review -> repeat/referral`

Every stage must have an owner, deadline, status, value, and measurable next action.

## 2. Business outcomes and operating targets

The first objective is not follower count. It is profitable, traceable bookings.

### North-star metric

**Monthly gross profit from marketing-attributed bookings**

Revenue alone can hide weak margins. Every campaign report should connect ad/content source to confirmed booking value, supplier cost, gross profit, and refund status.

### Initial 90-day targets

These are operating targets to validate and adjust after four weeks of real baseline data:

| Area | Initial target |
|---|---:|
| New enquiries acknowledged | within 5 minutes during working hours |
| Qualified enquiries receiving first useful response | within 15 minutes |
| Quotes followed up | 100% within 24 hours |
| Leads with source and campaign recorded | at least 95% |
| Leads with a next action/date | at least 95% |
| Enquiry-to-quote rate | at least 60% |
| Quote-to-booking rate | establish baseline, then improve 15% in 90 days |
| Lost leads with a recorded reason | at least 90% |
| Confirmed customers asked for a review | 100% |
| Marketing-attributed bookings with gross profit recorded | 100% |

### Twelve-month outcomes

- A reliable direct-enquiry engine not dependent on one channel.
- Strong visibility for high-intent local searches in Ras Al Khaimah and the UAE.
- A reusable audience of customers and opted-in prospects.
- A recurring corporate pipeline.
- Seasonal campaigns prepared six to eight weeks before demand.
- Paid media optimized to confirmed bookings and gross profit, not clicks.
- Repeat and referral business becoming a major source of low-cost bookings.

## 3. Brand and message architecture

### Mission

Kridiya helps UAE residents, families, pilgrims, and businesses plan and confirm travel with clear options and personal support, so they can travel with less uncertainty.

### Primary message

**Tell us where you need to go. Kridiya will check the practical options and personally guide you from quote to confirmation.**

### Supporting messages

| Customer need | Kridiya message | Proof to show |
|---|---|---|
| “Can I trust this agency?” | A real UAE travel company with clear quotations and reachable human support. | Legal name, RAK address, visible phone, business email, policies, real reviews |
| “Am I getting the right option?” | We compare suitable routes, stays, packages, and conditions for your actual needs. | Detailed quote, inclusions/exclusions, validity deadline, alternatives |
| “Will someone help if plans change?” | One advisor stays with the booking through payment, documents, confirmation, and support. | Named advisor, booking reference, status updates, handover documents |
| “Can you manage everything?” | Flights, hotels, visas, holidays, Umrah, cruises, and company travel in one conversation. | Service pages, bundled itineraries, customer examples |
| “Will you waste my time?” | Enquiry-first, fast-response service with no fake live-price claims. | Response commitment and honest availability language |

### Voice

Kridiya is **warm, trustworthy, clear, and energetic**.

- Use plain English, short sentences, exact inclusions, and calm urgency.
- Say “We will check and confirm” rather than implying unverified availability.
- Show price only with validity, inclusions, and conditions.
- Never claim “lowest price,” guaranteed visas, guaranteed approval, or live availability without evidence.
- Social content can be friendly; payment, visa, refund, and legal messages must be precise.

### Core calls to action

- **Get a personalised quote**
- **Chat with a travel advisor**
- **Send your travel dates**
- **Request a corporate travel account**
- **Check visa requirements**

“Book now” should be used only when an actual booking/payment path exists.

## 4. Priority audiences

### A. UAE residents visiting home

Typical needs: India, Pakistan, Philippines, and other home-country routes; baggage; family passengers; school-holiday demand; urgent changes.

Offer: route-and-date quote with baggage and change conditions clearly shown.

Best channels: Google Search, Google Business Profile, WhatsApp, Meta remarketing, referral.

### B. Families and couples taking holidays

Typical needs: Georgia, Azerbaijan, Thailand, Bali, Maldives, Turkey, Kerala, UAE staycations; bundled planning; visa support.

Offer: three package levels—Value, Comfort, and Premium—each with inclusions.

Best channels: Instagram, Facebook, short video, Google Search, email/WhatsApp lists, referral.

### C. Visa-assistance customers

Typical needs: document checklist, process clarity, realistic timing, status updates.

Offer: destination-specific assessment and checklist. Never imply approval is guaranteed.

Best channels: SEO, Google Search, educational social content, WhatsApp.

### D. Umrah travellers

Typical needs: respectful guidance, visa, flights, hotel proximity, transport, family or group arrangements.

Offer: clearly tiered packages with dates, hotel distance, transport, meal, and guide details.

Best channels: community referrals, WhatsApp, Meta, search, trusted local partnerships.

### E. Cruise customers

Typical needs: understanding ship, cabin, itinerary, visa, flights, hotels, and transfers.

Offer: complete cruise-planning call and land-plus-cruise package.

Best channels: inspirational video/social, search, remarketing, customer events.

### F. SMEs and corporate travel buyers

Typical needs: fast quotations, traveller profiles, approvals/LPOs, invoices, monthly statements, an escalation contact, and policy control.

Offer: **Kridiya Corporate Travel Desk** with a defined response SLA and account review.

Best channels: direct outreach, LinkedIn presence, referrals, local business groups, email, corporate landing page.

- corporate travel-spend review;
- repeat-customer priority response;
- referral benefit that is financially controlled and recorded.

### Lead qualification

Use a simple 100-point model after enough real data exists:

- travel within 30 days: +20;
- exact dates and route/destination supplied: +15;
- phone/WhatsApp verified: +10;
- realistic budget supplied: +15;
- repeat customer: +15;
- corporate account or recurring need: +15;
- responds to qualification question: +10.

The score helps prioritize; it must never replace staff judgment.

### Service-level rules

- Instant automatic acknowledgement.
- Staff accepts or assigns the lead within 5 minutes during working hours.
- Ask only the missing qualification questions.
- Give the customer the next-update time.
- Quote includes validity, inclusions, exclusions, conditions, and one clear CTA.
- Follow up at sensible intervals: same day when urgent; otherwise 24 hours, 72 hours, and a final close-the-loop message.
- Every lost lead gets one reason: price, timing, no response, unavailable, documents/eligibility, chose competitor, postponed, duplicate, or other.

## 7. Channel plan

### 7.1 Website and landing pages

The existing service pages are the correct foundation. Improve them as conversion destinations, not as a general brochure.

Required:

- one high-intent landing page per major service and important destination/theme;
- sticky WhatsApp/call CTA on mobile;
- short, service-specific forms;
- trust block near the first CTA;
- visible response-time expectation;
- FAQs based on real customer objections;
- no unverified prices or availability;
- thank-you page that explains what happens next;
- first-party campaign attribution saved with the enquiry;
- structured data where accurate;
- fast mobile performance and accessibility.

Priority landing-page clusters:

1. Flight tickets from UAE/RAK.
2. Holiday packages from UAE by destination.
3. Visa assistance by destination and customer nationality/residency context.
4. Umrah packages from UAE.
5. Cruise packages from UAE.
6. Corporate travel management in RAK/UAE.

### 7.2 Local SEO and reputation

- Fully complete and verify Google Business Profile.
- Keep name, address, phone, hours, website, and service information consistent.
- Publish genuine photos of the business, team, customer handovers, and approved travel moments.
- Request reviews after confirmed service or completed travel.
- Reply to every review professionally; never disclose travel or personal details.
- Build locally relevant pages and partnerships instead of mass-producing thin location pages.
- Track calls, website clicks, direction requests, reviews, and enquiry outcomes.

### 7.3 Search marketing

Search is the priority paid channel because it captures active intent.

Campaign groups:

- brand;
- flights by high-value route/theme;
- holiday packages by destination;
- visa assistance by destination;
- Umrah;
- cruise;
- corporate travel agency/management;
- remarketing/search audiences only after consent and sufficient volume.

Rules:

- send every ad group to the matching page;
- use negative keywords aggressively;
- separate research terms from buying terms;
- use call and lead assets during staffed hours;
- capture click IDs and import qualified lead and confirmed booking outcomes;
- optimize to qualified leads first, then confirmed bookings when volume supports it;
- review search terms weekly.

### 7.4 Meta, Instagram, and Facebook

Use Meta for discovery, proof, remarketing, and WhatsApp conversations.

Content mix:

- destination inspiration;
- practical checklists;
- real offer breakdowns;
- customer proof with permission;
- advisor/team presence;
- urgency tied to real deadlines;
- myths and mistakes;
- behind-the-scenes quotation and planning.

Paid campaign sequence:

1. Test destination/service creative with broad but relevant geography.
2. Use click-to-WhatsApp for offers that need conversation.
3. Retarget engaged visitors and viewers with proof and a direct quote CTA.
4. Exclude converted leads/customers from acquisition where practical.
5. Record conversation outcomes back in the CRM.

### 7.5 Email and WhatsApp

These are sales and retention channels, not uncontrolled broadcast lists.

Segments:

- enquiry but no quote;
- quote not decided;
- booked customer;
- travel completed;
- flight customer by frequent route;
- holiday interest by destination;
- visa interest;
- Umrah interest;
- cruise interest;
- corporate prospect;
- active corporate account;
- dormant customer;
- opted out.

Core sequences:

- enquiry acknowledgement;
- quote delivery and follow-up;
- payment/document reminders;
- booking confirmation and pre-travel checklist;
- post-travel check-in and review request;
- 60/90/180-day relevant repeat reminder;
- seasonal early-access campaign;
- corporate prospect follow-up.

Marketing broadcasts require a recorded lawful basis/consent and a clear opt-out. Operational booking messages must remain separate from promotional broadcasts.

### 7.6 Corporate acquisition

Build a named-account pipeline, beginning with RAK and nearby UAE SMEs.

Weekly process:

- identify 20 suitable companies;
- research travel need and correct contact;
- send a short, relevant introduction;
- offer a 20-minute travel-process review;
- follow up with a one-page corporate service summary;
- record decision-maker, billing contact, services, volume, policy, LPO, and payment terms;
- move qualified companies into proposal/onboarding.

Corporate content:

- how to control travel approvals;
- traveller-document readiness;
- urgent booking escalation;
- monthly statement and invoice workflow;
- cost visibility without compromising traveller care.

### 7.7 Partnerships and referrals

Potential partners: local businesses, HR/admin consultants, event planners, wedding planners, community organizations, education consultants, hotels, and complementary service providers.

Every arrangement requires:

- written scope;
- approved message and offer;
- trackable partner code/source;
- clear fee/benefit;
- no misleading visa or price claims;
- monthly lead, booking, gross-profit, and payout review.

## 8. Content operating system

3. **See the journey** — destinations, hotels, cruises, experiences.
4. **Meet the advisor** — real human expertise and process.

| Wednesday | offer/package with full context |
| Thursday | FAQ, myth, or customer objection |

Minimum monthly production:

- 4 offer posts;
- 4 educational carousels;
- 4 proof/team posts;
- 2 SEO articles or destination guides;
- 1 corporate article or case study;
- daily stories when there is something useful to show.

## 10. Paid-media budget framework

Do not commit a large annual budget before conversion tracking and response discipline work.

### Recommended test level

An initial **AED 6,000–10,000 monthly media budget** is a practical controlled test if the business can respond quickly and fulfil the demand.

Suggested starting allocation:

| Use | Share |
|---|---:|
| Google high-intent search | 50% |
| Meta click-to-WhatsApp/prospecting | 25% |
| Remarketing | 10% |
| Seasonal creative tests | 10% |
| Experimental/local partnership amplification | 5% |

This is a planning range, not a guaranteed spend recommendation. Start lower if staff capacity or measurement is not ready.

### Scale rule

Increase a campaign by no more than roughly 15–25% at a time only when:

- lead quality is acceptable;
- response SLA is met;
- confirmed bookings are recorded;
- gross profit covers acquisition cost at the required level;
- refund/cancellation risk is understood.

Pause or repair campaigns when leads cannot be handled, tracking is missing, supplier availability changes, or confirmed gross profit is consistently inadequate.

### Financial measures

- `CPL = ad spend / qualified leads`
- `CAC = ad spend / new confirmed customers`
- `ROAS = attributed booking revenue / ad spend`
- `Marketing gross-profit return = attributed gross profit / ad spend`
- `Contribution after marketing = attributed gross profit - ad spend - campaign-specific costs`

Use gross-profit return and contribution after marketing for final decisions.

## 11. Measurement and attribution

### Measurement stack

- GA4 for website acquisition and behaviour.
- Google Search Console for organic search.
- Google Ads and Meta Ads for platform delivery.
- Supabase CRM for lead, quote, booking, payment, cost, refund, and profit truth.
- A dashboard that joins campaign IDs to real outcomes.

### Events

Track:

- `view_service`
- `start_enquiry`
- `submit_enquiry`
- `click_whatsapp`
- `click_call`
- `click_email`
- `register_account`
- `login_account`
- `quote_sent` (CRM)
- `qualified_lead` (CRM)
- `payment_received` (CRM)
- `booking_confirmed` (CRM)
- `refund_completed` (CRM)

Phone and WhatsApp clicks are useful signals, but a confirmed CRM enquiry is the lead. A booking confirmed with payment/profit data is the business outcome.

### UTM convention

Use lowercase values:

`utm_source=instagram`
`utm_medium=paid_social`
`utm_campaign=2026_q4_georgia_family`
`utm_content=reel_family_itinerary_v1`
`utm_term=` only where relevant
`utm_id=kt-2026-041`

Never publish an untagged campaign link.

### Dashboard views

Daily:

- new enquiries by source/service;
- unassigned leads;
- first-response SLA;
- quotes due and overdue;
- spend and obvious tracking failures.

Weekly:

- qualified leads, quote rate, booking rate;
- CPL and CAC;
- lost reasons;
- performance by campaign, service, staff, and landing page;
- search terms and creative results.

Monthly:

- attributed revenue, supplier cost, gross profit, refunds, and net contribution;
- new vs repeat customers;
- customer lifetime/repeat indicators;
- channel mix and assisted conversions;
- budget reallocation decision;
- campaign learnings archived.

## 12. Retention, reviews, and referrals

### Post-booking lifecycle

- After confirmation: send clear handover and support contact.
- Before travel: send only relevant checklist/reminder.
- After travel: check that the journey went well.
- If satisfied: request a Google review with a direct link.
- With permission: request a testimonial/photo.
- Tag future interests and likely next travel window.
- Send relevant, infrequent offers—not generic blasts.

### Referral system

- Give each referral a code or referring-customer link.
- Define a controlled benefit after the referred booking is paid/confirmed.
- Record referral cost against gross profit.
- Prevent staff from making unapproved promises.
- Review fraud, duplicates, cancellations, and refunds before benefit fulfilment.

## 13. Roles and governance

One person may hold multiple roles initially, but accountability must remain explicit.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Owner | positioning, budget, financial thresholds, sensitive claims, monthly review |
| Marketing lead | campaign calendar, briefs, publishing, reporting, agency control |
| Sales/Enquiry lead | response SLA, qualification, quote follow-up, lost reasons |
| Operations | availability, supplier terms, fulfilment truth, customer handover |
| Finance | payment, cost, margin, refund, and campaign contribution data |
| Content creator | approved production and asset library |
| Technical/admin | tracking, forms, CRM fields, dashboards, access/security |

### Approval levels

- Routine evergreen content: marketing lead.
- Prices, packages, supplier claims, visa claims: operations approval.
- Discounts, referral benefits, and media budget changes: owner/finance approval.
- Customer stories/photos: documented customer permission.
- Corporate proposals and payment terms: owner/authorized manager.

## 14. Privacy, security, and quality controls

- Collect only the data needed for the stated purpose.
- Record consent separately from accepting website terms.
- Make promotional opt-in unbundled and optional.
- Provide a clear unsubscribe/stop mechanism and honour it promptly.
- Restrict audience exports to authorized staff.
- Do not upload passport or sensitive booking data to ad platforms.
- Use private storage and signed access for documents.
- Do not put customer details into design tools or shared creative briefs.
- Keep operational messages distinct from marketing.
- Maintain a campaign claims log with source, approver, validity, and expiry.

The UAE Government describes the federal Personal Data Protection Law as requiring governance and protection of personal data and generally prohibiting processing without consent except in specified cases. Legal review should confirm the exact implementation for Kridiya's activities:
https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws

## 15. 90-day implementation plan

### Days 1–15: foundation

- Approve positioning, audiences, service offers, and response SLA.
- Appoint owners for marketing, sales follow-up, operations approval, and reporting.
- Verify business facts and trust elements across the site and profiles.
- Audit GA4, Search Console, Google Business Profile, Google Ads, Meta, and WhatsApp Business access.
- Define UTM and campaign naming.
- Add campaign/source/click-ID/consent fields to the enquiry path.
- Define dashboard baseline and financial thresholds.
- Clean and segment existing customer/enquiry data lawfully.

**Gate:** no paid scaling until enquiries retain source data and staff can respond on time.

### Days 16–30: conversion and content

- Improve forms and thank-you path by service.
- Build the campaign brief, content calendar, offer template, and approval workflow.
- Prepare four weeks of content.
- Create review-request and post-travel workflows.
- Create Google Business Profile publishing/review routine.
- Build first flight, holiday, visa, Umrah, and corporate test campaigns.
- Train staff on qualification, CRM stages, next actions, and lost reasons.

**Gate:** test every campaign from click to CRM record to booking attribution.

### Days 31–60: controlled launch

- Launch high-intent search at controlled budgets.
- Launch two or three Meta click-to-WhatsApp creative tests.
- Begin corporate named-account outreach.
- Start weekly performance and lost-reason review.
- Improve landing pages from real enquiries and objections.
- Connect qualified lead and booking outcomes back to ad reporting where appropriate.
- Stop weak terms/creative; expand only proven combinations.

### Days 61–90: optimize and retain

- Compare source-to-gross-profit results.
- Launch remarketing only after audience/consent readiness.
- Introduce repeat-customer and referral workflows.
- Scale winning service/campaign combinations carefully.
- Build the next seasonal campaign six to eight weeks ahead.
- Publish the first corporate proof/case asset.
- Complete a privacy, access, attribution, and claims audit.
- Approve the next-quarter budget based on contribution, not impressions.

## 17. What not to do

- Do not buy followers, reviews, or unverified customer lists.
- Do not boost random posts without a campaign objective and tracking.
- Do not publish prices without conditions and validity.
- Do not optimize only to clicks, views, or WhatsApp opens.
- Do not run every service in one generic campaign.
- Do not scale spend while response or fulfilment is failing.
- Do not expose customer/passport/payment information in marketing tools.
- Do not create hundreds of low-quality SEO pages.
- Do not let an agency own accounts, pixels, domains, or data under its own credentials.
- Do not automate messages before the manual workflow is correct.

## 18. Owner scorecard

The owner should be able to answer these ten questions every month:

1. Which campaigns produced confirmed bookings?
2. Which produced the most gross profit after advertising?
3. How quickly did staff answer?
4. What percentage of enquiries received a quote and follow-up?
5. Why were leads lost?
6. Which services, destinations, and customer segments are growing?
7. How much came from repeat customers and referrals?
8. Which claims, offers, or supplier conditions expire next month?
9. Are consent, opt-out, access, and customer-data controls working?
10. What will be stopped, fixed, tested, and scaled next month?
